Channels & Creative Work

Creator, Editor & Publisher — Past Unseen

Self-run channel · YouTube & Facebook

Short-form Nepali history & heritage

A channel of vertical videos on Nepali history, heritage and the stories behind places people walk past every day — researched, scripted, edited and published entirely by me.

  • Research and script each episode in Nepali first, then build the edit around the narration so one idea lands per shot.
  • Generate scenes with AI video tools (Google Flow, Gemini, Grok) using shot-by-shot prompting for stories no camera ever recorded, then colour-match and cut them by hand.
  • Produce and sync Nepali voiceover, music and sound design, with ducking and cleanup so narration stays clear.
  • Add on-screen captions for sound-off viewing, and frame every shot inside platform safe areas.
  • Run both platforms for the brand myself — the YouTube channel and the Past Unseen Facebook page — adapting the cut, captions and posting rhythm to each.
  • Best-performing episode reached 38K views and 3.2K reactions.

Creator & Editor — Bijok Ramailo (Comedy Nepal)

Self-run page · Facebook

Nepali comedy · animal-led short-form reels

Everyday Nepali situations — payday running out, guests at the door, Dashain on a budget — acted out with animal footage, where a reaction delivers the punchline faster than dialogue.

  • Built a repeatable format for the page: hook in the first two seconds, one clear idea, animal reaction as the punchline, clean end frame.
  • Designed the end frame as brand-ready space, so a product, logo or call to action can sit in a reel without breaking the joke — the same structure works for sponsored content.
  • Edit vertical and caption in Nepali and English, so a video reads across age groups and with the sound off.
  • Top reels reached 94K and 90K views, with 4.4K and 1.6K reactions.

How I Work

  • Self-taught and still learning. Nobody assigned this to me — I picked it up because I wanted to understand what makes a video hold someone’s attention, and I have been teaching myself ever since, one video at a time.
  • Learn by building. Every new tool, transition or AI model gets tested on a real video rather than read about, and the feed gives honest feedback on whether it worked.
  • End to end. Concept, script, visuals, edit, sound, captions, thumbnail and publishing — no handoffs, so the idea stays intact from the first line to the export.
  • Made in free time. Evenings and weekends, after the code is closed for the day — which is exactly why the quality bar is my own.
